02 · Labs

What each lab is about

Lab 1

Tuesday 20 Oct
09:30 to 13:00

Lab lead
Prof. Dr. Sebastian Weck

SCADA and Power System Operation

You take the seat of the grid operator. On h_da's grid training simulator you work with a real network control system covering 400 kV, 110 kV and 20 kV levels: reading the state overview and load flows, planning switching sequences, and carrying them out by the book. Clear, uniform language matters: information calls, switching calls, and the handover of disposition authorization between the control centre and the work crew on site.

Exercises

  • Reading the grid. Network loading, losses and loss cost per hour; P and Q of the Nordost 400 coal plant; loading and losses of the West 400 to Nord 400 line; busbar voltages in West 20.
  • Circuit breaker maintenance. Take a line out of service in Mitte 20 following the five safety rules, set work markers, and transfer disposition authorization to the local work responsible.
  • Busbar isolation. Move all feeders and the transformer in UW Ost 20 from busbar SS1 to SS2 without interrupting supply.
  • Transformer changeover. Parallel TR69 and TR70 in West 20 (voltage difference below 300 V), move the earthing coils, isolate and earth the transformer for maintenance.
  • Parallel transformers. Offset tap positions and observe the reactive circulating current.

The worksheet for exercises 1 to 4b is in the files below.

Lab 2

Wednesday 21 Oct
10:30 to 14:00

Lab leads
Prof. Dr.-Ing. Thomas Betz
Frank Radler

High Voltage Technology: breakdown strength in air

A modular high-voltage kit, a 100 kV test transformer, a 100 pF measuring capacitor and a 50 kΩ series resistor. You build the test circuit yourselves, have your teammates check it, and then raise the voltage until the air gap breaks down. The aim is to see how electrode geometry and voltage polarity shape the field and the breakdown voltage.

Experiments

  • Part 1a: AC, different geometries. Breakdown voltage Ûd versus gap for plate-plate (10 to 60 mm) and point-plate (30 to 150 mm). Compute mean field strength, geometry factor p = (s + r)/r, utilization factor η and maximum field strength, with an equivalent point radius of 0.5 mm.
  • Part 1b: Pre-breakdown phenomena. In a darkened room, record glow, streamer and spark inception voltages for a point-point gap of 150 mm.
  • Part 2: DC polarity effect. Add rectifier diodes and a 10 nF DC capacitor; measure breakdown and partial discharge inception for positive and negative point-plate configurations and explain the difference.
  • Demonstration: lightning impulse. The multi-stage impulse voltage generator applies 1.2/50 µs lightning impulses to a railway mast disconnector (RPS TracFeed). See how the stages charge in parallel and discharge in series, and read front time, time to half value and chopping from the recorded impulse. Background in the lightning impulse handout.
  • Demonstration: Tesla transformer. A 400 V supply, a 14 kV transformer and a 2 kV spark gap drive two tuned resonant circuits to a high-frequency high voltage that lights a fluorescent tube without wires. A small solid-state coil plays music through its spark. The handout shows why the gap fires 28 times per 50 Hz cycle.
  • Experiment: partial discharges in solids. A needle on the high-voltage contact inside solid insulation produces small discharges long before breakdown. Observe where the pulses sit on the sine wave, the polarity effect and how electrical trees grow. Background in the Tesla and partial discharge handout.

Safety first. Before the experiment your supervisor introduces the earthing equipment. You may only start after the safety briefing and after confirming it with your signature.

The English introduction and the lightning impulse and Tesla/partial discharge handouts are in the files below.

Lab 3

Thursday 22 Oct
09:30 to 13:00

Lab lead
Prof. Dr.-Ing. Athanasios Krontiris

Power System Protection

Protection is only as good as the signals it receives and the settings it is given. This lab follows both: first the current transformer that feeds the relay, then the distance relays that decide which breaker trips.

Current transformers

  • Measure the CT winding resistance and burden impedances with a Thomson bridge.
  • Direct measurement of the composite error to IEC 61869-2 with rated burden, overloading and underloading, including phase displacement from the oscilloscope.
  • Indirect measurement, and the effect of saturation and burden on accuracy.

Distance protection in a medium-voltage network model

  • Set and test an electromechanical SD 34A distance relay in a radial network.
  • Coordinate a KU relay with the SD 34A.
  • Set and test a digital SIPROTEC 7SA511 on a parallel line and in a ring network.

03 · Report

Lab report, in groups of two

Writing session: Fri 23 Oct · 09:00 to 12:00
With Dr. Petros Aristidou

Submit on Moodle by

Sunday 1 November 2026

One PDF per group of 2 students

Download report template (.docx)

Friday morning is reserved for writing up the labs together while the measurements are still fresh. Work in groups of two; both students are jointly responsible for the whole report.

Submission

  • One submission per group on Moodle (EEN442), uploaded by one group member
  • A single PDF named EEN442_Lab_Surname1_Surname2.pdf
  • Both names and student IDs on the first page
  • Deadline: Sunday 1 November 2026

What the report contains

  1. Part 1: High Voltage (max. 3 pages). Setup, AC and DC breakdown tables with Emean, p, η and Emax, breakdown and PD inception curves, analysis of field uniformity and the polarity effect.
  2. Part 2: Protection (max. 4 pages). CT errors to IEC 61869-2 against accuracy class 1, effect of burden and security factor FS, distance protection test results and selectivity.
  3. Part 3: SCADA (max. 2 pages). Network readings, two switching sequences with the five safety rules and DA handover, short discussion.
  4. Appendices. Photographs and raw measurement tables, not counted in the page limits.

Bring on Friday

  • Your filled-in measurement tables and notes from all three labs
  • Any photos of the test setups and simulator screens
  • A laptop with your plotting tool of choice
